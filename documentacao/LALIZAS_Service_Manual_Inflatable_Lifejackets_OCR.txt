2. INSPECTION

2.1 Visually inspect all webbings, buckles, stitching and cover materials looking for signs of wear or
damage. Any defect, which could affect the performance or the safety of the lifejacket, should be noted
along with the serial number of the lifejacket, and the lifejackets should be marked as unsuitable for use.
Also check for abrasion and puncture marks.

2.2 Record the serial number on the record sheet (See SECTION 9).

2.3 Open up the cover and unfold the bladder. Visually inspect the bladder (including all of its
components), bladder attachment, stitching for any signs of wear or damage. Any defect, which could
affect the performance or the safety of the lifejacket, should be noted along with the serial number of the
lifejacket, and the lifejacket should be marked as unsuitable for use.

Extra care should be taken with lifejackets with integral harness, as to the condition of the harness
stitching, especially around the stainless steel D ring area.

2.4 Remove the gas cylinder by unscrewing it from the inflation mechanism and check that the end seal
is intact. Then, inspect it visually for any signs of damage or corrosion. The threads should be in good
condition, and there should be no evidence of “scouring” on the burst cap. If there are any signs of
damage or wear the cylinder should be replaced with a new one. Also, noting the presence of the green
ready for action tags.

2.5 If all of the above is deemed to be acceptable, then continue the servicing procedure.

NOTE: Do not re-fit the cylinder until after the lifejacket has been pressure-tested. The existing or the
replacing cylinder should be “weigh-checked” using a set of calibrated digital scales with accuracy
capable of reading up to 1/10 of a grammar. You should ensure that it is within the minimum gross
tolerance as marked (segregated cylinders should be emptied of their gas contents in a safe manner prior
to disposal).

Especially for the Epsilon inflatable lifejacket, the cylinder’s permissible tolerances are stamped on the
side of the inflation unit.

There is no recommended life-span for CO. cylinders. Provided they pass the weight test and that they
are not corroded or damaged, they can remain in service.

3. PRESSURE TEST
Note: Before start the tests, record the room temperature at the area of the lifejacket.
3.1 Pressure Test Chamber

3.1.1 Inflate the lifejacket, using the oral inflation tube, up to 220 mbar and maintain it. Then reduce the
pressure to 200 mbar.

3.1.2 After having recorded the temperature & the pressure of the lifejacket, leave the lifejacket for 5
minutes.

3.1.3 Record again the pressure of the lifejacket and the room temperature.

3.1.4 If the temperature’s change is 0.5 degrees Celsius or less, continue to step 3.1.5. If the
temperature’s change is greater than 0.5 degrees Celsius, then the test shall be considered void and
should be repeated.

3.1.5 Leave the lung inflated for a period of 4 hours in an area of constant temperature.

3.1.6 After 4 hours check again the pressure. If the pressure has dropped by 5% or less, the lifejacket
shall be considered to have PASSED the test.

3.1.7 Then the lifejacket should be recorded as passed and placed into an area clearly marked ‘PASSED
TEST’.

3.1.8 If the pressure has dropped by more than 5% or less, the lifejacket will have deemed to have
FAILED the test. Then, the lifejacket must be inspected for any leakage, as described in Section 4.

3.1.9 Then the lifejacket shall be labelled accordingly and placed into an area clearly marked ‘FAILED
TEST’.

3.1.10 If the lifejacket has passed the test, then fully deflate the bladder, ensuring all residual air is
removed.

3.1.11 To deflate the lifejacket, remove the cap on the oral inflation tube and insert the nipple into the
valve head. Gently squeeze the lifejacket to expel the remaining air or CO2. When fully deflated, place
again on the cap of oral tube.

4, FINDING LEAKS

4.1 Remove the inflator mechanism with the 14mm socket, using the torque wrench set to 3 N.M.

4.2 Using a mix of approx 500 parts water to one part liquid soap, apply the mix over the surface of the
inflated bladder and watch carefully for air bubbles forming. After leaks have been marked, soapy water
should then be rinsed off using clean fresh water. Alternatively, the inflated bladder can be submerged in
a tank of clean fresh water and observations made for a column of air bubbles appearing.

4.3 If the leak is found to be from any part of the bladder material or welded seam, the lifejacket should
be deemed as FAILED and not suitable for use.

4.4 If a leak is located from the inlet valve, replace the valve and then retest the lifejacket in accordance
with the method for that bladder type.

NOTE: A record should be made of all lifejacket tested including date, serial number, PASS or FAIL (if
failed include a note of failure) and name of the Inspector. If the lifejacket is deemed to have failed,
inform the customer and advise the lifejacket is no longer suitable for use.

4.5 Lifejackets deemed to have passed Inspection & Pressure Test should be dried off and are then ready
for re-arming and packing.

4.6 For the Epsilon 150N inflatable lifejacket, you don’t have to remove the inflation mechanism.
However, the leakage test can be conducted only by spaying a soap and water mix (see $4.2).

NOTE: Leakage testing by immersion the Epsilon 150N lifejacket in a tank will lead to its inflation (full
function).

DEFLATE LIFEJACKET

4.7 Ensure that a record of the lifejacket serial number, any parts used and other relevant comments are
kept for each lifejacket.

4.8 Ensure that all the residual air is removed from the lifejacket. Insert a nipple on the cap strap and
hold into the valve. Apply gentle pressure to the lifejacket and deflate it. Ensure that the cap is in the
normal position on the inflation tube.

Automatic systems have a manual system as well as a water activation unit. Before the automatic unit is
checked, first read and understand the manual servicing instruction at section 5.1.

To check the automatic system Lalizas, first inspect the inflator cap making sure the green “ready for
action” indicator is visible in the base of the unit.

Next remove and inspect the seal of the CO, cylinder. If the cylinder seal has bean pierced or the “red
fired” indicator is visible in the base of the unit the complete water activated device will need replacing.

To replace the water activated device, unscrew the inflator cap in an anticlockwise direction and discard
the used water activated device. Then install a new water activated device in the “inflation body” and
screw on the inflator cap in a clockwise direction until no thread is visible. Then check that the green
“ready for action” indicator it appears in the base.

Check the manual system “green tag” is in place and refit the CO, cylinder.

The replacing cylinder must be for:

© The adult lifejackets SOLAS Lamda 150N, SOLAS Delta, ISO 12402 Alpha, ISO 12402 Sigma, ISO
12402 Delta & ISO 12402 Ypsilon: 33gr cylinder.

e . The children’s lifejackets ISO 12402 Alpha: 22gr cylinder.
© The lifejackets SOLAS Lamda 275N & ISO 12402 OMEGA 275 N: 60gr cylinder.

NOTE: LALIZAS S.A. recommends that in every annual inspection of the lifejacket, the water
activated device (code 00340) should be replaced.
